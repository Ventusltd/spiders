# Spider DNA

The Spider is an executable metaphor.

It begins as a creature in language, but it becomes real when the metaphor decides folders, files, workflows, schemas, pages and recovery behaviour.

A Spider is not one dashboard.

A Spider is a way of seeing an estate as a living topology.

A Spider may be pointed at repositories, grid assets, data sources, project surfaces, market signals, live pages, audit reports or future species.

The first Spider was born in the federation map repository. That repository remains the first hibernation and recovery chamber.

This repository is the species lab.

Here the Spider DNA becomes reusable.

A species is a Spider form with a particular habitat, topology and behaviour.

The Federation Spider sees repository dependency topology.

A future Grid Spider may see physical grid topology.

A future Market Spider may see time-series and price topology.

A future Seer Spider may see all Spider species and route between them.

The DNA has five core laws.

First, the map is metadata.

The Spider must not clone the world into itself.

It should read manifests, cartridges, declared records, public surfaces and reproducible outputs.

It should not become the territory it maps.

Second, unknown is a real state.

A grey node is honest when evidence is missing.

A fake green node is a lie.

Third, declared and derived are different states.

Declared means a source, repo, principal or committed artefact states the fact.

Derived means a scanner, workflow or model inferred the fact from observable structure.

Derived findings may guide attention, but they must not dress themselves as declared truth.

Fourth, evidence is not proof.

A signal may be smelled.

An observation may be seen.

A chart may be drawn.

But proof requires reproducible read-back and a clean evidence trail.

Fifth, the Spider may see widely but claim narrowly.

It can reveal topology, risk, degradation, absence and motion.

It must not certify engineering truth unless a future verified professional layer explicitly carries that authority.

This DNA lets fiction cross into architecture.

The Spider is real because another executor can follow it.

The Spider is real because it can be versioned, tested, recovered and audited.

The Spider is real because it changes what the code is allowed to do.
